# Incident: Slip in Dish Pit

Owner: Grace Liu (FOH Lead)

## 1) Incident Summary (verbatim)

- A dishwasher slipped near the pre-rinse sink during a Saturday rush. Minor bruising; no lost time.
- Fix: anti-slip mats installed; floor drain cleared; wet-floor signs kept at the pit.

## 2) Purpose and Scope

- Prevent any repeat slips in or near the dish pit and pre-rinse sink.
- Define FOH actions during and after an incident, plus daily prevention steps.
- Applies to Front of House leads, servers, bussers, and support who enter/exit the dish pit pass-through or run glass racks to dish.

## 3) Immediate Response: Slip, Trip, or Fall in Dish Pit

1. Stop and secure the area:
   - Block traffic to the pit entrance. Post 2 wet-floor signs at both approaches.
   - If water is pooling, squeegee toward the floor drain. Do not walk through standing water.
2. Check the person:
   - Ask if they can move safely. For minor bruising and no lost time, assist to a chair and offer ice.
   - If head strike, heavy bleeding, severe pain, or they cannot stand: call the MOD and 911.
3. Notify:
   - Call the MOD and Grace Liu (FOH Lead).
   - BOH Dish or Prep Lead confirms drain status and source of water.
4. Make safe:
   - Place anti-slip mats flat with edges locked. Remove curled or damaged mats.
   - Keep wet-floor signs in position until the surface is fully dry and stable.
5. Clean and dry:
   - Degrease if the spill is soapy/greasy, rinse, then squeegee dry. Use absorbent pads for stubborn puddles.
6. Document:
   - MOD completes an Incident Report with: who, where (pre-rinse sink), time window (Saturday rush), injury (minor bruising; no lost time), cause suspected, corrective actions (mats installed, drain cleared, signs stationed).
7. Return to service:
   - Only reopen the pit pass-through when the floor is dry, mats are seated, and signs remain posted at the pit.

## 4) Root Causes to Check and Fix

- Overflow at pre-rinse sink from high spray or clogged food trap.
- Slow floor drain or blocked grate creating pooling.
- Missing, curled, or bunched mats.
- Grease/soap film from rinse aid, detergent, or fryer pans.
- Carry-over water from racks or bus tubs set on the floor.
- Traffic congestion during rush making detours around signs.

## 5) Daily Prevention Checklist (FOH in coordination with Dish)

- Pre-open
  - Confirm wet-floor signs are staged at the pit entrance.
  - Inspect anti-slip mats: flat, interlocked, no tears or curling edges.
  - Lift mat edges and check the floor drain grate is seated and clear of solids.
  - Dry-run squeegee path to drain; make sure tools are reachable (squeegee, mop, absorbent pads).
- During service
  - Post a wet-floor sign any time water is on the floor, even briefly.
  - Keep bus tubs and glass racks off the floor; use racks and shelves only.
  - Call out “wet floor, dish!” when moving through an active wet zone.
  - If pooling appears, pause traffic, squeegee to drain, then dry.
- Close
  - Pull mats, scrub, rinse, and hang to dry; scrub floor with degreaser, rinse, and squeegee dry.
  - Clear the drain basket and replace it; confirm no standing water.
  - Return dry mats flat; stage 2 wet-floor signs at the pit.

## 7) Mat and Floor Care Procedure

1. Remove debris with a scraper; lift mats carefully—don’t drag.
2. Scrub mats with hot water and degreaser per label; rinse thoroughly.
3. Hang to dry until drip-free; do not reinstall wet mats.
4. Inspect edges and holes; replace torn or curled sections immediately.
5. Clean floor: apply degreaser, brush, rinse, squeegee to floor drain.
6. Verify floor drain basket is clear; reinstall grate.
7. Lay mats flat, interlock edges, confirm no trip lips.
8. Stage two wet-floor signs at the pit at all times.

## 8) What To Do If Things Aren’t Right

- Drain is slow or backing up:
  - Pull the drain basket, clear solids, flush with hot water. If still slow, call MOD to schedule a snaking before next service. Keep wet-floor signs posted until resolved.
- Mats won’t sit flat:
  - Flip/rotate; if still curled, pull from service and replace. Do not tape down.
- Recurring slick film:
  - Check for detergent or rinse-aid overspray at the pre-rinse sink. Wipe, rinse, and degrease the area. Verify chemical dispensers aren’t leaking.
- Water keeps pooling from rack returns:
  - Add a drying pause at the dish machine exit; place a bus towel on the rack landing (swap when saturated).
- Injury worse than minor bruising:
  - Stop work, call 911 and the MOD. Do not move the person unless in immediate danger. Preserve the scene for review.

## 9) Communication and Training

- FOH Lead briefs the team pre-shift on current floor status and any hazards.
- New hires are shown: pre-rinse sink location, mat layout, floor drain, squeegee path, and wet-floor sign locations.
- After any incident, FOH Lead walks the correction list: anti-slip mats installed; floor drain cleared; wet-floor signs kept at the pit, and confirms everything is in place before the next rush.

## 10) Acceptance Criteria (Pass/Fail)

- Floor around pre-rinse sink is dry to the step with mats flat and interlocked.
- Two wet-floor signs are visible at both approaches to the dish pit.
- Floor drain grate and basket are in place and free of solids.
- No standing water for more than 1 minute during rush; pooled water triggers squeegee and signage immediately.
- Incident is documented by MOD with noted injury: minor bruising; no lost time.
