# Food Safety Basics: Temperature Danger Zone

Owner: Samuel Okafor (Sous Chef, Prep)

Summary: Danger zone 41–135°F; cold holding ≤ 41°F; hot holding ≥ 135°F; reheat to 165°F.

## 1) Why This Matters on Prep
- The prep station sets the baseline for safe food all day. If we keep food out of the danger zone, service runs clean and no one gets sick.
- Bacteria grow fastest between 70°F and 125°F. That is why the first stage of cooling (135°F → 70°F) is the tightest.
- Keep it cold when it should be cold, and reheat right when needed for hot holding. Measure, don’t guess.

## 2) Key Temperature Rules (Do Not Deviate)
| Rule | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Danger zone | **41°F – 135°F** |
| Cold holding | 41°F or below |
| Hot holding | 135°F or above |
| Reheating for hot holding | 165°F within 2 h |
| Poultry, stuffed foods | 165°F |
| Ground beef | 155°F |

- If you are not sure a food hit its target, temp it again. When in doubt, pull it from service and call Samuel.

## 3) Tools and Set-Up
- Digital probe thermometer with alcohol wipes
- Shallow hotel pans; sheet pans; wire racks; cambros with lids
- Ice and bus tubs for ice baths
- Labels and markers
- Walk-in shelving clear for airflow; do not stack hot pans tight
- Sanitizer bucket and clean towels at the station

## 4) Cold Holding on Prep
- Keep TCS foods (protein, dairy, cut produce, cooked starches, sauces) at 41°F or below.
- Actions:
  1) Store prepped items in shallow pans or cambros with headspace for airflow.
  2) Work in small batches. Only bring out what you can prep fast, then return to cold holding.
  3) Use ice baths for mixing cold emulsions or salads that warm up on the table.
  4) Keep lids on between use. Close lowboys and reach-ins promptly.
  5) Temp check any item you suspect warmed up. If it reads in the danger zone, see Section 8.

## 5) Cooling Cooked Foods on Prep
- Goal: Move food through the danger zone fast, especially 135°F → 70°F.
- Actions:
  1) Immediately after cooking, spread product in shallow pans. Do not cool in deep cambros.
  2) Vent steam. Use a rack or tilt the lid to release heat.
  3) Use an ice bath: pan set into a bus tub with ice and a little water; stir to speed heat loss.
  4) Stir frequently with a sanitized utensil; scrape sides to break heat pockets.
  5) Space pans in the walk-in. Do not stack hot pans. Leave lids ajar until cold holding temp is reached.
  6) Label when cooling starts. Move to covered storage once at cold holding temperature.

## 6) Reheating for Hot Holding (Prep to Service Transfer)
- When reheating a batch item for hot holding, bring it to 165°F within 2 h.
- Actions:
  1) Reheat quickly on stove or combi; do not “warm” slowly.
  2) Stir and temp in the center and the thickest spots.
  3) Hold at 135°F or above once reheated. If it drops, reheat back up promptly.
- Cook temps to verify before service when applicable:
  - Poultry, stuffed foods: 165°F
  - Ground beef: 155°F

## 7) Thermometer Use and Checks
- Clean and sanitize the probe before and after each use.
- Insert into the thickest part, avoiding the pan bottom and bones.
- For mixed items (soups, sauces), stir and take two readings in different spots.
- If a reading seems off, recheck immediately. If still off, switch to a backup thermometer and tell Samuel.

## 8) What To Do If Temps Are Out
- Cold item above 41°F:
  - If recently prepped and still on the table: return to cold holding immediately or rapid-chill in an ice bath. Retemp before storing.
  - If found in the walk-in above target: move to an ice bath or shallow pan and chill fast; notify Samuel.
  - If time in the danger zone is unknown: pull from service and call Samuel.
- Hot item below 135°F in holding:
  - Reheat to 165°F quickly, then return to hot holding at or above 135°F.
  - If you cannot reheat immediately, pull from service and notify Samuel.
- Cooling item stuck warm:
  - Split into smaller pans, refresh the ice bath, and stir. Check airflow in the walk-in. Retemp and document.
- Equipment issues (warm lowboy, empty ice bin, broken thermometer): stop, protect product (move to safe temp), and alert Samuel.

## 9) Daily Prep Station Checklist
- Before prep:
  - Thermometer sanitized and reading consistently
  - Ice bath set-up ready
  - Walk-in space cleared for shallow pans
  - Labels and markers at hand
- During prep:
  - Work in small batches; keep lids on; return items to cold holding fast
  - Temp check suspect items; document when asked
- After cooking/cooling:
  - Shallow pans, vented; ice bath as needed; spaced in walk-in
  - Cover and store once at cold holding temperature
- Before handoff to service:
  - Reheated batch items at 165°F within 2 h
  - Hot holding at 135°F or above
  - Cold items at 41°F or below
- Communicate any issues immediately to Samuel Okafor (Sous Chef, Prep).
