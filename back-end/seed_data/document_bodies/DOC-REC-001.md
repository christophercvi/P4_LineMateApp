# Smoked Brisket (Texas style)

Owner: Priya Nair (Sous Chef, Grill)

Summary: 14-hour post-oak smoke, salt-and-pepper rub, wrap at 165°F, hold at 150°F.

## Core Specs (do not change)
- Yield: 1 packer brisket (12–14 lb raw) ≈ 26 portions of 6 oz.

Rub
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Kosher salt | 1 part |
| 16-mesh black pepper | 1 part |
| Granulated garlic | ¼ part |

Method
1. Trim fat cap to ¼ in. Rub the night before; hold uncovered at 41°F or below.
2. Smoke at 250°F with post oak until internal hits 165°F and bark is set (≈ 8 h).
3. Wrap in butcher paper; continue to 203°F probe-tender (≈ 4–6 h).
4. Rest 1 h, then hold in the Alto-Shaam at 150°F. Discard after 6 h in hold.

Service
- Slice against the grain, pencil-thick. Point and flat are portioned separately.

## Cut, Trim, and Rub
1. Use one whole packer brisket, 12–14 lb raw. Identify the flat and the point; note the grain runs in different directions.
2. Square ragged edges; remove hard deckle fat. Leave a clean aerodynamic shape.
3. Trim fat cap to ¼ in. across the flat; leave a little more on the point if thin areas need protection.
4. Mix rub: 1 part kosher salt, 1 part 16-mesh black pepper, ¼ part granulated garlic. Make enough to coat all surfaces (about ½ cup total per 12–14 lb brisket).
5. Light coat of neutral oil optional only on bare meat spots to help adhesion; do not over-oil.
6. Season all sides evenly. Press to adhere. No sugar in rub.
7. Place on wire rack, sheet pan below. Rub the night before; hold uncovered at 41°F or below to dry-brine and set the surface.

## Smoke Setup and Timeline
- Wood: Post oak only. Use clean-burning splits; avoid smolder.
- Pit temp target: 250°F at grate level. Stabilize before meat goes on.
- Probe placement: Insert a leave-in probe in the thickest part of the flat, tip centered.
- Pan of hot water in pit if needed to stabilize humidity and bark formation.

Cook schedule guide (actual times vary by size and pit draft):
| Stage | Pit Temp | Internal Target | Approx Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start to bark set | 250°F | 165°F | ≈ 8 h |
| Wrapped finish | 250°F | 203°F probe-tender | ≈ 4–6 h |
| Rest | Off pit | Carryover settles | 1 h |
| Hot hold | Alto-Shaam | 150°F | Up to 6 h (then discard) |

Notes
- “Probe-tender” means a thermometer probe slides in with butter-like resistance in flat and point around 200–203°F; go by feel at 203°F.
- Expect a stall around 150–170°F. Do not raise pit temp above 275°F.

## Wrapping, Resting, and Holding
1. When internal hits 165°F and bark is set (doesn’t scrape off with a fingernail), wrap tight in two layers unwaxed pink butcher paper. Keep seam on top.
2. Return to 250°F and cook until 203°F probe-tender (≈ 4–6 h).
3. Pull from pit. Vent wrap 2 minutes to stop carryover. Close wrap and rest 1 h on a rack.
4. Move wrapped brisket to the Alto-Shaam at 150°F. Place seam up in hotel pan to catch juices.
5. Label start of hot hold. Discard after 6 h in hold.

If not needed for service (do not hot-hold):
- Keep wrapped. Vent briefly, then chill on sheet pan: 135°F→70°F within 2 h, 70°F→41°F within 4 more h. Store covered at 41°F or below.

## Service and Slicing
- Separate point from flat by following the fat seam after the rest.
- Slice against the grain, pencil-thick. Turn the point 90° to stay against grain.
- Portion: 6 oz cooked weight per order. Keep slices from flat and point separate.
- Dip the cut face briefly in collected juices if slices look dry. Do not soak.

## Grill Station Checklists
Pre-smoke
- Brisket trimmed, rubbed, on rack, uncovered at 41°F or below overnight.
- Post oak splits staged, pit cleaned, thermometers calibrated in ice water at 32°F.
- Butcher paper, towels, hotel pans, probes, gloves ready.

During smoke
- Maintain clean fire: thin blue smoke, vent wide open, no white billow.
- Log pit temp and internal every hour. Mop not required; do not spritz if bark is forming well.
- Rotate once if pit has hot spots; keep fat cap up if heat is mostly from below.

Hold and service
- Verify Alto-Shaam 150°F with a thermometer. Track 6 h hold limit.
- Slice to order; keep unsliced portions wrapped to retain moisture.

## Troubleshooting and What To Do If
- Bark too light at 165°F: Delay wrap 30–60 min until bark is set. Do not exceed 275°F.
- Bark too dark early: Check for dirty smoke. Reduce wood load, open vents, add dry split for cleaner burn.
- Stuck in stall past 10 h: Confirm probe accuracy. If pit steady at 250°F, wrap and push to finish; do not raise above 275°F.
- Underdone at 203°F (still tight): Keep wrapped and continue in 10–15 min increments until probe-tender.
- Overdone (crumbly slices): Switch to thicker slices; prioritize point meat; use ends for chopped applications only if not in hot hold over 6 h.
- Running late: After wrap, you can finish in 250°F convection oven to target; wood flavor is already set.
- Running early: Rest 1 h, then hot-hold at 150°F. Do not exceed 6 h in hold; discard after 6 h in hold.

## Food Safety and Quality Notes
- Keep raw brisket at 41°F or below before cooking. Avoid temperature abuse during rub and load.
- Minimum hot-hold is 135°F; we hold at 150°F for quality and safety. Discard after 6 h in hold.
- Use clean gloves for slicing. Sanitize board every 4 hours or when switching tasks.
- Never reuse butcher paper from raw or cooked product. Replace if torn or soaked through.
