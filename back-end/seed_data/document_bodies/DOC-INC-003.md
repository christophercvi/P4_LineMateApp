# Incident: Allergen Near-Miss, Table 12

Document owner: Grace Liu (FOH Lead)

## 1) Incident Summary (Original, unedited)
- A salad with candied pecans was plated for a guest with a tree-nut allergy. Expo caught it at the pass because the ticket was flagged ALLERGY.
- Root cause: the modifier printed on a second line and was missed at garde manger.
- Fix: POS modifier moved to the top line in bold; allergen SOP refresher at pre-shift.

## 2) Purpose and Scope
- Prevent any allergen reaching a guest when an order is flagged ALLERGY.
- Standardize FOH steps from greeting to handoff at the pass.
- Clarify expo checks and communication with garde manger and the rest of the line.
- Applies to all Front of House staff: servers, runners, expo, hosts; interfaces with all stations.

## 3) Immediate Response Procedure (when an ALLERGY is declared)
1) Acknowledge: Repeat back the allergy to the guest. Example: “Noted—tree-nut allergy.”
2) Clarify: Ask what happens on exposure and confirm cross-contact sensitivity.
3) Mark POS: Use the ALLERGY modifier; itemize the specific allergen in the modifier notes.
4) Verbally call: At send, call “ALLERGY: [item], [allergen]” to expo.
5) Allergen ticket: Hand the printed chit to expo; do not park it anywhere else.
6) Runner plan: Assign a runner; runner repeats back the allergy to expo.
7) Final verify at pass: Expo reads ticket and plate label out loud; FOH confirms.
8) Table drop: Server repeats the allergy on drop. No garnish swaps at the table, ever.
9) Follow-up: Check back early; confirm guest is safe and satisfied.

## 4) POS Entry Standards (to prevent missed modifiers)
- Always select the global ALLERGY modifier plus type the specific allergen (e.g., “tree nuts”).
- One ALLERGY per allergic guest; list affected items clearly.
- Do not bury the allergen note in free text; use the standard modifier first.
- Confirm the modifier preview shows at the very top line in bold before sending.
- If the ALLERGY is not showing on top or looks split across lines, stop and alert expo to reprint before the kitchen starts the dish.
- Never ring “no nuts” without the ALLERGY flag.

## 5) Expo and Pass Checks (Zero-Miss Checklist)
- Ticket shows ALLERGY on the top line in bold, legible, not cut off.
- Read-back: Expo states, “ALLERGY—[allergen]—[item], no [garnish/allergen].”
- Visual scan: Compare plate to the no-go list for that allergen (see table below).
- Garnish audit: Confirm no secondary garnishes or dusts that could contain the allergen.
- Separate landing: Place allergy plates on a clean, designated pass zone.
- Runner confirmation: Runner repeats the allergen back to expo before leaving the pass.

## 6) Allergen Callouts and Common Risk Garnishes
| Allergen callout (FOH) | Common risk on salads/apps | Hidden sources to ask BOH about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tree nuts | Candied pecans, almonds, walnuts | Pesto, romesco, nut oils, crumble mixes |
| Peanuts | Peanut crumble, satay | Chili crisp, sauce bases |
| Dairy | Cheese, crema, butter brush | Buttermilk dressings, aioli |
| Eggs | Mayo-based dressings | Fresh pasta, batters |
| Gluten/Wheat | Croutons, breadcrumbs | Fryer cross-contact, soy sauce |
| Sesame | Seeds, tahini | Toasted seed mix, buns |
| Shellfish | Shrimp, oyster sauces | Fish sauce blends |
| Soy | Tamari, tofu | Marinades, emulsions |

Note: For tree nuts, pay special attention to candied nut garnishes and any “crunch” components.

## 7) What To Do If Something Goes Wrong
- If you see a risk on the plate at the pass:
  - Say “Stop—ALLERGY plate—possible [allergen] present.”
  - Pull the plate from service; hand it to expo. Do not try to pick off garnishes.
  - Expo notifies the station; remake is prioritized.
  - FOH informs the guest that we are remaking the dish to keep them safe.
- If the ticket prints without ALLERGY on top:
  - Do not send it down the line. Reprint or rewrite clearly, then hand to expo.
  - Notify a manager and the POS lead to check the printer format.
- If the guest adds an allergy after ordering:
  - Void and re-ring with ALLERGY properly flagged; verbally call to expo.
  - Confirm remake; do not modify the existing plate at the pass or table.

## 8) FOH–BOH Communication Flow
- Host to server: Note allergy on the handoff and in seating notes when known.
- Server to expo: Verbal call plus physical chit handoff on every ALLERGY order.
- Expo to stations: Read the allergy out loud; confirm understanding with garde manger or relevant station.
- Runner to server at window: “ALLERGY—[allergen]—this plate is for Table [#].”
- Server at table: “This is your [item], prepared with your tree-nut allergy in mind.”

## 9) Root Cause and Corrective Actions (Original, unedited)
- Root cause: the modifier printed on a second line and was missed at garde manger.
- Fix: POS modifier moved to the top line in bold; allergen SOP refresher at pre-shift.

## 10) Verification and Training
- Pre-shift: Quick allergen SOP refresher led by expo or the shift lead; review any menu changes that affect allergens.
- Shadow checks: New FOH staff complete two observed ALLERGY orders with expo sign-off.
- Spot audits: Managers pull random chits to confirm ALLERGY prints on the top line in bold.
- Feedback loop: Expo logs any catches; FOH reviews at the next pre-shift to prevent repeats.

## 11) Service Tips to Prevent Near-Misses
- Slow down on ALLERGY entries; deliberate beats fast.
- Never assume a garnish is “safe.” Ask if unsure.
- Keep the pass uncluttered; allergy plates need a clean, obvious landing zone.
- Speak in clear, loud, calm calls. ALLERGY calls come first at the pass.
