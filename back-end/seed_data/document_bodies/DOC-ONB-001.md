# Knife Safety & Handling — Prep Station

## Owner & Purpose
- Document owner: Samuel Okafor (Sous Chef, Prep).
- Purpose: prevent cuts, slips, and cross-contamination at the Prep station.
- Applies to all knives, mandolines, oyster knives, and cutting boards used in Prep.
- Read, follow, and ask Samuel if anything is unclear.

## Core Rules (Do Not Deviate)
1. **Claw grip** — fingertips tucked, knuckles guide the blade.
2. Put a **damp towel** under every cutting board so it can't slide.
3. **Never catch a falling knife.** Step back and let it drop.
4. Carry knives point down, blade facing back, and call "**Sharp, behind!**".
5. Never leave knives in the sink or under water. Wash, dry and return to the magnetic strip.
6. Cut-resistant glove on the guiding hand for mandolines and oysters.
7. A dull knife is dangerous: hone before every shift; sharpening service every two weeks.

## Station Setup & Board Control
- Set board on a clean, folded damp towel so it can't slide.
- Choose the right board size; keep a trash cambro and garnish pan within reach.
- Keep blade path clear: no towels, cords, or pans crossing the board.
- Wipe and swap boards if the surface grooves or becomes greasy.

## Safe Carry & Traffic Calls
- Move with knives at your side: point down, blade facing back.
- Call “Sharp, behind!” before stepping into traffic or passing a teammate.
- Never carry more than one bare blade per hand; use a rack or tray for multiples.
- Place knives down before opening doors, moving carts, or grabbing hot pans.

## Washing, Storage & Tool Control
- Never leave knives in the sink or under water.
- Wash, rinse, sanitize, then dry immediately; no air-drying blades.
- Return to the magnetic strip with handles aligned, tips up and safe.
- If a magnet is full, use guards and store in the designated knife drawer.

## Sharpness Standards & Service
- A dull knife is dangerous: hone before every shift; sharpening service every two weeks.
- Test sharpness with a tomato or paper slice; if it skids or tears, re-hone or pull from service.
- Use the honing steel at a steady angle; avoid grinding away the edge on boards.

## How To Hone (Quick Steps)
1. Wipe blade clean and dry.
2. Hold steel vertical, tip planted on a towel.
3. Set 15–20° angle; draw heel to tip, light pressure.
4. Alternate sides, 5–8 passes each.
5. Wipe blade; test on tomato or paper. Repeat if needed.

## Mandolines, Oysters & Gloves
- Cut-resistant glove on the guiding hand for mandolines and oysters.
- Use the mandoline guard; never freehand small ends—switch to knife.
- For oysters: stabilize towel, keep the other hand gloved and behind the tip.
- Store mandolines with guards on; blade covered when not in use.

## What To Do If...
- Knife falls: do not reach—step back, warn “Knife down!”, then pick up safely.
- Board slides: stop cutting; reset with a damp towel under the board.
- You get a cut: stop, wash, pressure, bandage with finger cot/glove, tell Samuel.
- Knife is dull or chipped: tag “DULL/CHIP,” pull from the line, log for service.
