# Walk-in Cooler Temperature Alarm Response

> Illustrative content — verify against your local health code.

Owner: Samuel Okafor (Sous Chef, Prep)

## 1) Purpose and Scope
- For Prep station leads and cooks responding to a walk-in cooler alarm at or above 41°F.
- Goal: protect TCS food, keep product safe for service, and get refrigeration fixed fast.
- Applies to the main walk-in used by Prep; coordinate with Grill, Pastry, and Front of House if shared product is affected.

## 2) Immediate Response — Step-by-Step
1. Stop what you’re doing. Bring a calibrated probe thermometer and the temperature log.
2. Check the door — gasket, strip curtain and door closer. Most alarms are a propped door. Close it fully. Remove any carts or pans blocking the closer.
3. Read the display and a probe thermometer in a product (not air). Log both.
4. If product is above 41°F, follow the decision table below. Move TCS food as directed and call the refrigeration vendor.
5. Open a Critical ticket for the refrigeration vendor with the alarm time and readings.
6. Tell the Kitchen Manager before the next service.
7. If any TCS food is discarded, complete an incident report and update the waste log.

## 3) Temperature Checks and Logging
- Use a sanitized probe thermometer. Measure internal temp of a representative TCS product (e.g., sliced turkey, cooked beans, dairy). Do not measure air.
- Take at least two products from different shelves; log the highest product temperature and the walk-in display reading.
- Record:
  - Alarm time
  - Walk-in display temperature
  - Product name and location (shelf/rack)
  - Product temperature (°F)
  - Action taken (moved, discarded, held)
  - Your initials
- Keep the log on the Prep clipboard by the walk-in door.

## 4) TCS Food Decision Table (Time/Temp Actions)
| Situation | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Product at or below 41°F | Keep in place. Re-check in 30 minutes. | Ensure door fully seals; limit door openings. |
| Product above 41°F, under 4 hours | Move TCS food to the reach-ins / blast chiller, call refrigeration vendor. | Separate raw/ready-to-eat during transfer. Space pans for air flow. |
| Product above 41°F for 4 hours or more, or unknown | Discard TCS food and document on an incident report. | When in doubt about time, discard. |

- TCS examples in Prep: cooked grains/beans, cut melon, cut tomatoes, washed/cut leafy greens, cooked proteins, dairy, eggs, pooled sauces, house dressings.
- Use timers or production labels to verify elapsed time. If labels are missing or unclear, treat as unknown.

## 5) Vendor Contact and Ticketing
- Vendor: PolarServ Refrigeration — 24/7 line on the door of the walk-in.
- Open a Critical ticket for the refrigeration vendor with the alarm time and readings.
- Provide:
  - Alarm time and current walk-in display temperature
  - Highest product temperature and product type
  - What was already done (door checked, product moved)
  - Any noises, icing, or compressor short cycling noted
- After the call, place a “Service Called” tag on the walk-in handle.

## 6) Product Movement and Setups
- Reach-ins/blast chiller:
  - Pre-chill empty shelf space if possible. Do not overload; leave gaps between pans.
  - Use shallow pans (2 in) to help re-chill product faster.
  - Cover and label items with original prep labels.
- Staging:
  - If blast chiller is full, rotate the coldest items back into the walk-in only if at or below 41°F and door integrity is restored; otherwise use reach-ins.
  - Do not ice down sealed foods unless ice is part of the recipe or container is designed for ice bath. If using an ice bath to re-chill, maintain ice-to-water contact up to product line; keep food above ice water level.

## 7) Communication and Documentation
- Tell the Kitchen Manager before the next service.
- If discard is required:
  - Discard TCS food and document on an incident report.
  - Note items, quantities, and reasons in the waste log.
- Flag any par levels affected so ordering can adjust. Inform Grill, Pastry, and Front of House if menu items are impacted.

## 8) Troubleshooting and What-To-Do-If
- If the alarm clears after door correction:
  - Keep logging every 30 minutes for 2 hours to confirm recovery.
- If the door won’t seal:
  - Inspect gasket for tears, debris; clean and reseat. Check strip curtain alignment. Verify door closer speed; remove anything wedged in the jamb.
- If temps keep rising:
  - Minimize door openings, move TCS food as per the table, and escalate to the Kitchen Manager.
- If power outage is suspected:
  - Keep door closed. Check breakers only if trained. Treat time above 41°F as unknown unless continuously logged.
- If you can’t confirm time above 41°F:
  - Treat as unknown; discard TCS food and document on an incident report.
- If condensate or icing is visible:
  - Note it in the ticket; do not chip ice off coils. Keep airflow paths clear.

## 9) Preventive Practices for Prep
- Daily walk-in quick check at opening:
  - Door gasket intact, strip curtain hanging correctly, door closer pulls door shut from 6 in.
  - Nothing stored against evaporator or blocking airflow.
  - Thermometer log up to date; probe thermometer calibrated (ice slurry ~32°F).
- During prep:
  - Cool hot foods to 41°F properly before storing; use shallow pans and leave lids ajar until at 41°F.
  - Label all items with prep time; this supports the 4-hour decision point.
- End of shift:
  - Consolidate pans to reduce open product; don’t overpack shelves.
  - Verify door is closed, latched, and no carts are left propping it.

This SOP keeps product safe by acting fast on door issues, verifying actual product temperatures, following the 41°F and 4-hour limits for TCS food, documenting actions, and getting PolarServ Refrigeration on site when needed.
